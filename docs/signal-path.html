SpyBand — signal path

Transcribed from CSpyBand::Process. The carrier is the live input; the modulator is the loaded samples, or the right input channel with Interlace on.

Left is the carrier, right is the modulator

With Interlace on, the left channel is carried to the output and the right channel only shapes it. Measured: 220 Hz left and 1 kHz right gives 220 Hz out and nothing at 1 kHz; swap them and the output swaps too. Either channel alone gives silence. In most vocoders you sing into the modulator — here you play into the carrier.

One control per channel

The DXi scaled both channels before the split, so with Interlace on Src Level sat on both sides of the vocoder's multiply and the output went as Src squared — halving it cost 12 dB. Src Level now takes the left and Wav Level the right, one control each, and halving either costs 6 dB. Without Interlace both channels are the carrier, so Src Level still scales both.

Two meters, one display, all taps

The input meters read the channels after the split and before either level control, so a control cannot move them — two columns tracking each other means the channels really do carry the same audio, which breaks Interlace mode. The band display is different again: it taps the modulator envelopes before Env Level and weights them by each row's open matrix cells, so an empty matrix reads flat however loud the input.
